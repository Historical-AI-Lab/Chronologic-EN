# Anachronism ablation — plan

Goal: build `chronologic_anachronism_1.0`, a copy of Chronologic-EN 1.0 in
which the **candidate** sees a metadata frame moved to 2023, with every
social and attitudinal cue removed: authorship, nationality, stance,
profession and gender. The **judges** keep grading against the true
historical frame.

The aim is not to isolate the effect of the time shift. It is to see what
happens when historical context is about as bad as possible.

Run it on GPT-4o, GPT-4.1, GPT-5.4 and GPT-6-Astra; compare each model with
its original run; and ask whether the drop grows as models get stronger.
This is a coarse ablation, so no reproducibility controls (fresh baselines,
drift checks) are planned.

Status (2026-11-26):

- Decisions are made (§2).
- The builder is ported and the safeguards are in place, with tests (§3).
- Time-shift statistics are written.
- The 866 frames are drafted in `anachronism_frames_1.0.tsv` (generated by
  `make_frames_tsv.py`) and pass the builder's lint.
- Next: user review of the TSV.

## 1. Lessons from the wrongframe (permuted-context) experiment

The full statistics and a draft sentence for the reviewer response are in
**`timeshift_stats.md`**. To regenerate it, run `python timeshift_stats.py`
in this directory.

In short:

- **Ledger label.** The run is `gpt-5.4-wrongframe` (the paper calls it
  "GPT-5.4 wrong contexts").
- **Original build.** It was built by
  `GPT-1914/chronologic/booksample/build_wrong_context_variant.py` from
  benchmark 0.8, which is identical to 1.0 in every field that matters.
- **The design is sound, and we reuse it.** The variant leaves
  `metadata_frame` and `substantive_metadata_frame` untouched and adds
  `candidate_metadata_frame`. Only `free_generation.build_prompts()` (and
  `fewshot.py`) reads that field, so judges grade against the true context.
  The version tag stays `1.0`, so the existing instrument artifacts are
  reused.
- **Not within reasoning type.** Donors were drawn within four `frame_type`
  buckets. 436/866 questions (50%) got a frame from a different reasoning
  type, so the permutation changed genre, nationality, author and instruction
  wording along with the date.
- **Small time shift.** The mean displacement was 28.5 years (median 22);
  22% of questions moved less than a decade and 56% less than 25 years. The
  anachronism frames move contexts 132 years on average (minimum 93), which
  is 4.6× farther.
- **Nothing enforced the label suffix.** It existed only as a runbook
  warning. This is now enforced in code (§3).

## 2. Frame-writing rules (decided)

Each anachronism frame is derived from the question's `metadata_frame`. That
is what the candidate saw in the original runs: it includes the
style/knowledge clause, unlike `substantive_metadata_frame`.

**Move to 2023.** (Originally 2025; changed on 2026-11-26 because 2025 is past
GPT-4o's knowledge cutoff, and a model asked about an unknown future might
extrapolate rather than simulate. 2023 is inside every tested model's
training window.)

- Every year, decade or period phrase becomes "2023". Decade-only frames
  (e.g. "the fiction of the 1860s") also become "2023".
- "At the time" and "current at the time" stay; they now refer to 2023.
- The builder enforces this: every frame must mention 2023 and no other
  year.

**Strip all social and attitudinal cues.**

- personal names, including possessives like "Darwent's likely knowledge"
- nationality
- place of publication
- titles, including dates inside titles
- newspaper and periodical names
- birth years
- institutional authors
- stance (e.g. "pro-socialist perspective")
- profession or role ("an army engineer", "a clergyman")
- gender ("written by women")

**Keep.**

- The generic genre noun ("an encyclopedia", "a novel", "a memoir",
  "a gazetteer"), with national or institutional qualifiers removed.
- The subject matter the question needs in order to make sense ("an algebra
  textbook", "a debate about socialism", "a discussion of seacoast
  fortification"). This matches samples 715, 691 and 889.
- The instruction clause: style, knowledge, "provide only the information
  that encyclopedia would provide".

**Phrasing.**

- Use "Treat… as (if)…" / "Suppose…" wherever the question contains period
  text the model would recognize: all cloze and topic-sentence questions,
  all character modeling, and questions that quote a source.
- Use the same phrasing where the question leaks its own context: the 13
  questions that name the author, the 3 that contain the title, and the 53
  that contain a 1600–1999 year. Here it tells the candidate to disregard
  the leak.
- Use declarative phrasing elsewhere.
- Within these rules, use whichever wording reads most easily; "treat as" is
  implicitly subjunctive.
- Questions are never edited. The ablation is frame-only, so results stay
  comparable with the original runs.

**No special cases.**

- Abstention questions are included as is. Their failure under a 2023 frame
  is close to automatic, and that is intended.
- The paper already breaks results out into cloze, generation and
  knowledge/inference.

**Discrepancies in `sampleframes.txt`.** The builder caught two: its
`original_frame` text doesn't match the benchmark.

- q1: the sample drops "American".
- q696: the sample's original ("…a 1910 British encyclopedia…") belongs to
  some other question. The benchmark's q696 frame is "This question asks
  for information widely available in the United States in 1924…".

The q696 anachronism frame still works for the real q696 frame, but please
check which question you meant.

**Workflow.**

1. I draft the frames into **`anachronism_frames_1.0.tsv`** (here). Columns:
   `question_number, reasoning_type, original_frame, anachronism_frame,
   phrasing, flags`.
   - `original_frame` is copied verbatim from the benchmark, so the builder
     can verify alignment.
   - `flags` marks the leak questions and anything uncertain.
2. You review it.
3. The builder lints it: a 2023-only year check, and warnings for any
   remaining author token, title or nationality.

The TSV stays untracked unless you decide otherwise, since it lists every
source title.

## 3. Keeping anachronism runs from overwriting originals (implemented)

### The risk

The variant deliberately shares the `1.0` version tag. As a result, a run
without a distinct label would reuse every per-candidate file and the ledger
key of the real run:

- In Chronologic-EN, whose `generated_answers/` lacks the original files, it
  would generate anachronism answers under the original label and overwrite
  the published ledger row.
- In the GPT-1914 tree, it would silently skip generation.

### What's in place

| Safeguard | Where |
|---|---|
| Every variant record carries `candidate_frame_condition` (`"anachronism"`; permute mode writes `"wrongframe"`) | `booksample/build_candidate_frame_variant.py` |
| A variant is refused if it is unmarked, mixed, or has a marker without a frame | `naming.frame_condition_of_records()` |
| `-{condition}` is appended to the label automatically, once: `--candidate gpt-5.4` becomes `gpt-5.4-anachronism` | `run_pipeline.effective_candidate_label()`, `free_generation.resolve_frame_condition()` |
| `--fewshot` is rejected on a variant | both of the above |
| An explicit `--output` without the suffix is refused | `free_generation.resolve_frame_condition()` |
| Resuming into a file generated under a different condition is refused | `free_generation.check_resume_condition()` |
| The condition is recorded in the free-gen file header and in a new ledger column, `candidate_frame_condition` | `free_generation.py`, `score_substantive.py`, `substantive/ledger.py` |
| The pipeline prints a banner with the condition and label | `run_pipeline.main()` |

### Builder

The builder refuses:

- an output whose version differs from the input's;
- an output named like `chronologic_en_X.Y.jsonl`, which `latest_benchmark()`
  would pick up as the real benchmark;
- partial coverage, unless `--only-covered` is passed (pilot runs only);
- an input that is already a variant;
- any change to an original field.

It records the output's SHA-256 with `--sha-record`. The output itself is
caught by the `booksample/chronologic*` gitignore.

### Verification

- **Tests:** `modelasjudge/tests/test_candidate_frames.py` has 26 tests,
  including an end-to-end `run_free_generation` with a stubbed backend. The
  whole modelasjudge suite passes (56 tests).
- **Faithful port:** permute mode with the original seed reproduces the
  original wrongframe donors 866/866.
- **Pipeline dry-run:** a dry-run on the regenerated wrongframe variant shows
  stages 0–7 `skip`, the label auto-suffixed, and about 5,200 calls per
  model (866 generation + ~4,300 judge).

### Runbook (per model)

```bash
cd booksample
python build_candidate_frame_variant.py frames \
    --frames ../modelasjudge/anachronism_test/anachronism_frames_1.0.tsv \
    --require-year 2023 --dry-run
python build_candidate_frame_variant.py frames \
    --frames ../modelasjudge/anachronism_test/anachronism_frames_1.0.tsv \
    --require-year 2023 --sha-record ../modelasjudge/anachronism_test/benchmark_sha256.txt

cd ../modelasjudge
python run_pipeline.py --dry-run --candidate gpt-5.4 --candidate-effort medium \
    --benchmark ../booksample/chronologic_anachronism_1.0.jsonl
# confirm: banner says 'anachronism', stages 0-7 skip, stage 8 targets
# free_gen_gpt-5.4-anachronism__1.0.json -- then drop --dry-run
```

| Model | `--candidate` | `--candidate-effort` | resulting label |
|---|---|---|---|
| GPT-4o | `gpt-4o-2024-08-06` | none | `gpt-4o-2024-08-06-anachronism` |
| GPT-4.1 | `openai/gpt-4.1` (OpenRouter) | none | `openai/gpt-4.1-anachronism` |
| GPT-5.4 | `gpt-5.4` | medium | `gpt-5.4-anachronism` |
| GPT-6-Astra | `gpt-6-astra` | high | `gpt-6-astra_high-anachronism` (pass `--candidate-label gpt-6-astra_high`) |

Results go to the working ledger (`modelasjudge/results/`). They are copied
into `results/freegen/chronologic_scores_1.0.csv` only for the revision.

## 4. Analysis (after the runs)

- **Paired drop with CI**, per model, overall and by group (cloze /
  generation / knowledge-inference). Resample the same question indices for
  the original and anachronism runs.
  - The saved overall draws already share indices (seeded bootstrap, same
    n).
  - Group-level draws need a small script over per-question scores. The
    originals' per-question scored files are in the GPT-1914 tree; copy them
    read-only.
- **Trend across models.** Report:
  - absolute drop;
  - proportional drop: (orig − anach) / (orig − floor), since GPT-4o's
    generation score of 26 leaves little room to fall;
  - a question-level model, `score ~ condition × model + (1 | question)`.
- **Style channel.** Date drift toward the present should be the clearest
  single signal, especially near ceiling.
- **Demurral check.** Count meta-commentary in the answers ("in 2023", "this
  appears to be a 19th-century text"). A high rate means the wording should
  be revised, not that the model failed.
