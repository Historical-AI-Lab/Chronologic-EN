# Temporal displacement: wrongframe vs. anachronism

Generated by `timeshift_stats.py` from `chronologic_en_1.0.jsonl` (866 questions; source dates 1603–1930, median 1897). Displacement = |year of the frame the candidate saw − true source year|. For wrongframe, the frame's year is the donor question's source date; for anachronism it is 2023.

## Headline

| | mean | median | SD | 10th–90th pct | min–max | share < 10 yrs | share < 25 yrs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| wrongframe (permuted) | 28.5 | 22 | 28.0 | 4–62 | 0–321 | 21.7% | 56.4% |
| anachronism (2023) | 132.0 | 126 | 27.7 | 103–171 | 93–420 | 0.0% | 0.0% |

- The anachronism frames move contexts **4.6×** farther on average than permutation did (132 vs. 28.5 years), and never by less than 93 years. Under permutation, 22% of questions moved less than a decade and 56% less than 25 years.
- Permutation was roughly symmetric: 49% of donors were later than the true source, 50% earlier, 1% the same year (signed mean +0.0 yrs). Anachronism always moves forward.
- Permutation drew donors within four `frame_type` buckets (world / character / other book / passage), **not** within reasoning type: 436/866 questions (50%) got a frame from a different reasoning type. So the wrongframe frames also changed genre, nationality, author and sometimes the instruction wording, not just the date.

## Draft sentence for the reviewer response

> In the permuted-context ablation, the frame a model saw was displaced from the true source date by 29 years on average (median 22; 22% of questions by less than a decade), because all but 3 of the 866 sources fall between 1831 and 1930. The anachronism ablation moves every frame to 2023: a mean displacement of 132 years (minimum 93), about 5× larger, and it also strips authorship, nationality, stance, profession and gender cues from the frame.
